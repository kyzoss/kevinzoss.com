Chapter one · Solon, Ohio

It started behind a food counter.

I learned how a business actually runs from behind a counter — inventory, rushes, waste. Then I built Feed Cleveland, a nonprofit that routed surplus food from restaurants and grocers to food banks. First system I ever built, and it set the pattern for everything after: find what's being wasted, design the route, move what matters.

Chapter two · Columbus

100,000 people. One feeling.

Game on the line, and the whole place comes apart at once — attention at full volume. Weeknights I was building the other side of it: a Cavs site and a music blog that aggregated everything I could get my hands on. Teaching myself how an audience gets built instead of just standing in one.

Chapter three · Hermosa Beach → Los Angeles

Then the whole city opened up.

Started in a Hermosa apartment and ended up working across the whole basin. New horizons, agency side — learning from the inside how the largest brands on earth actually operate, then spending my days reinventing how they did it with mobile technology most of them hadn't figured out yet.

Chapter four · Venice

We built ad tech in a beach house.

Mobile Theory, then Aki — building mobile ad tech out of Venice beach houses, back when a startup here was a rented house with the boardwalk out front and the skate bowl below. We sold Aki. That's the chapter where I stopped renting other people's systems and started building my own.

Chapter five · El Segundo

These days it ships without me.

Co-founded Lazy Boy Agency, running media and technology — the buys out front, the systems underneath. AI teams ship overnight: client tools, and side projects like Stretch Quest, a game that gets kids to actually do their physical therapy. The rest of the time I'm in this yard with my family, which is the whole point of building things that run without you.
