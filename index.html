Chapter one · Solon, Ohio

It started behind a food counter.

Five jobs before I could legally buy a beer, every one of them behind a counter. Then I built Feed Cleveland, a nonprofit that moved surplus food from restaurants and grocers to food banks. Same instinct ever since: find what's being wasted and route it where it matters.

Chapter two · Columbus

100,000 people. One feeling.

Game on the line, and the whole place comes apart at once — my first real lesson in what attention feels like at scale. Weeknights I was building websites: a Cavs fan site and a music blog that aggregated everything I could get my hands on. First taste of building an audience instead of just sitting in one.

Chapter three · Hermosa Beach

Then I traded snow for saltwater.

Lived in Hermosa, worked in agencies — Saatchi & Saatchi on Toyota, then Horizon across Comedy Central, FilmDistrict, Corona, Jack in the Box, Rdio and a long list after that. Learning the craft of buying attention on somebody else's dime, at real scale, with real consequences.

Chapter four · Venice

We built ad tech in a beach house.

Mobile Theory, then Aki — building mobile ad tech out of Venice beach houses back when that's just how startups here worked, boardwalk out front and the skate bowl below. Then we sold Aki. That's where I stopped only buying attention and started building the machinery that delivers it.

Chapter five · El Segundo

These days it ships without me.

Co-founded Lazy Boy Agency, where I run media and technology — the buys out front, the tech underneath. AI teams ship overnight, this site included. The rest of the time I'm out here with a young family, which is the actual point.
